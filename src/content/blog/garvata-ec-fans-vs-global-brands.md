## Energy, not purchase price, decides the cost

A fan in a 24x7 AHU, at an IT park, a pharma plant or a data center, runs 8,760 hours a year. At ₹10 per unit, that adds up quickly:

| Fan input power | Energy per year (24x7) | Cost per year at ₹10/unit | Cost over 10 years |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 kW | 17,520 kWh | ₹1.75 lakh | ₹17.5 lakh |
| 3 kW | 26,280 kWh | ₹2.63 lakh | ₹26.3 lakh |
| 5 kW | 43,800 kWh | ₹4.38 lakh | ₹43.8 lakh |

A fan that draws **5% less power** at your duty point saves about ₹13,000 a year per 3 kW fan, or ₹1.3 lakh over ten years. Across 40 fans in an IT park, that is about ₹52 lakh. So the first number to ask any supplier for is **input power at your airflow and static pressure**, not the price per fan.

## What every good EC plug fan has in common

The core technology is shared across the leading suppliers:

- **EC (electronically commutated) motors** with permanent magnets and integrated electronics. Leading designs are rated **IE5-equivalent**: benchmarked against the IE5 class for variable-speed motors in IEC TS 60034-30-2. The standard itself excludes motors built into a fan that can't be tested on their own, which is why makers say "equivalent".
- **Backward-curved plug impellers** that discharge directly into the AHU plenum, with no scroll housing, belts or pulleys.
- **0–100% speed control** built in, with no separate VFD.
- **Modbus RTU** communication over RS485, so speed can be commanded and status read by a BMS or controller.
- **Dynamic balancing** of the impeller and motor to grade G6.3 under ISO 21940.

ebm-papst's RadiPac tender specification, for example, covers all of these. If a supplier can't offer them, keep looking.

## Questions to ask any EC fan supplier

1. What is the input power at my airflow and static pressure, and how was it measured (ISO 5801 or AMCA 210)?
2. What is the maximum ambient temperature at full load, and does output derate above it?
3. What supply voltage range does it tolerate without derating?
4. Which spares are stocked in India, and what is the lead time for a replacement fan?
5. Who commissions it, and who comes when it fails?
6. What can it report over Modbus: power, speed, temperature, faults, vibration?
7. What will control the fan speed after installation, and who sets it up?

Ask every supplier the same questions and compare the cost over ten years, not the price on the quote.
